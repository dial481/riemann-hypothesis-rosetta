# Community reading survey, 21 November 2026

**The closest match to Rosetta’s first reading note is §10 of Siniša Slijepčević’s outline.** It describes the odd-rank congruence limit and the use of two theta lifts in even rank. It also says where the author could not reconstruct the geometric seed. Rosetta’s source citations for `GeoOne`, `SeedFinOdd` and `exists_limit_rep` give a mathematician precise statements to compare there. Resolving the geometric construction itself remains a substantive reading problem. ## Earlier publications found in this pass

- **Rudy Pei’s Nov 20 roadmap** explicitly flags the gap between some interface names and the local statements supplied to them. Its proposed Galois/local reading target is a natural companion to Rosetta’s `Row0b` clauses. Pei discloses AI assistance and bases the roadmap on a source reading. The roadmap’s estimates of mathematical novelty use a different definition from Rosetta’s archive-provenance classification. [Roadmap and write-up targets](https://peabrane.github.io/post/riemann-hypothesis-lean-roadmap/)
- **Robin Zhang’s preliminary note** traces possible literature antecedents. Section 5 concerns denominator control by Galois cohomology and offers a place to look for attribution when reading the Selmer estimates. Zhang describes the draft’s AI assistance and limits on its fidelity to the Lean development. [Preliminary note, version 1](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00809v1)
- **Manvir Jaswal’s Nov 21 guide** gives declaration references for a path across all five endpoints and compares classical inputs with the archive’s formulations. Its scope is helpful when a theorem appears in a five-endpoint roadmap but has no witness in an RH-only trace. [Guide, especially §§6–7](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00799v1)
